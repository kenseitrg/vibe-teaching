## Main message

Modern broadband seismic aims for 6+ octaves of usable bandwidth (e.g., 1–64 Hz) by overcoming ghost notches that limit conventional marine data. Marine broadband techniques focus on low-frequency extension (deghosting), while land broadband must address both source and receiver limitations. The benefits include improved wavelet sharpness, low-frequency texture, interpretation accuracy, deep imaging, and AVO/inversion.

## Key points

### 1. Introduction to broadband (pp. 27–29)

- **Ghost notches:** reflection at sea surface creates notches in amplitude spectrum, limiting bandwidth at both low and high ends.
- **Deghosting:** process of removing ghosts to extend bandwidth.
- **Target:** 6+ octaves of usable bandwidth (e.g., 1–64 Hz).
- **Historical context:** earlier methods focused on high-frequency extension; modern methods emphasize low-frequency extension.
- **Land challenge:** achieving marine-style broadband on land is difficult due to source and receiver limitations, but recent developments show 6 octaves is achievable.

### 2. Benefits of broadband data (pp. 27–29, after Denis et al. 2013)

- **Wavelet:** resolution and interpretability proportional to number of octaves. With 6+ octaves, wavelet becomes sharp and impulsive; low frequencies minimize side lobes.
- **Low-frequency texture:** subtle impedance variations become visible; layers have distinctive signatures; faults more easily tracked.
- **Interpretation accuracy:** broadband wavelet simplifies images, reveals subtle details, highlights impedance variations from fluid/lithology.
- **Deep imaging:** low frequencies less affected by absorption; improve imaging beneath complex overburdens (salt, basalt, chalk).
- **AVO and inversion:** removal of ghost provides more consistent AVO behavior; extended low-frequency bandwidth reduces dependence on a priori model (only 0–3 Hz gap needed).

### 3. High-frequency broadband (pp. 29–30)

- **Bandwidth extension:** methods claim to recover frequencies beyond Nyquist or not generated by source.
- **Caution:** must validate with well synthetics; some methods simply "flatten" spectrum without adding information.
- **Validation:** only way to validate is comparison to well synthetic data; check if additional events tie to real geology.
- **Resolution misconception:** increasing highest frequency does not necessarily increase resolution; resolution depends on **average frequency** (lobe width $\propto 2/(F_{\max} - F_{\min})$).

### 4. Bandwidth and resolution (pp. 30–32)

- **Lobe width equation:**
  $$L_w \propto \frac{2}{F_{\max} - F_{\min}}$$
- **Average frequency:** increasing $F_{\min}$ increases average frequency, narrowing lobe width.
- **Side lobes:** determined by **relative bandwidth in octaves**, not absolute bandwidth.
- **Example:** 10–20–80–100 Hz wavelet has wider lobe than 30–40–80–100 Hz wavelet, but broader bandwidth wavelet has much lower sidelobes.
- **Key insight:** high frequencies control resolution (central lobe width); low frequencies control isolation (side lobe amplitude).

### 5. Offshore broadband techniques (pp. 32–37)

- **Ghost notch frequency:**
  $$F_N = \frac{1500 \cdot N}{2D}$$
  where $N$ is integer, $D$ is depth (source or receiver), 1500 m/s is water velocity.
  
  First notch at $N = 0$ is always 0 Hz. Second notch at $N = 1$ is $F_1 = \frac{1500}{2D}$.

- **Commercial techniques:**
  - **GeoStreamer® (PGS):** dual-sensor (hydrophone + geophone). Hydrophone responds with opposite polarity to ghost, geophone with same polarity. Sum cancels ghost. Limitation: geophone S/N low at low frequencies; summation typically above 10 Hz.
  - **Sentinel® MS (Sercel):** similar to GeoStreamer.
  - **IsoMetrix (Schlumberger):** hydrophones + accelerometers; measures vertical and horizontal components; allows 3D wavefield deghosting and crossline interpolation.
  - **BroadSeis™ (CGG):** variable-depth streamer; receiver ghost notch varies along cable; "notch diversity" exploited by processing to remove ghosts. Cable depth/shape can be tuned for different targets.

- **Variable-depth streamer concept:**
  - Flat streamer: ghost delay time varies with offset/angle (after NMO, ghost time is constant for flattened event).
  - Variable-depth streamer: ghost notches are function of streamer depth after moveout; different depths give different notch frequencies; processing combines to fill notches.

### 6. Evaluating broadband results (pp. 37–40)

- **QC:** compare amplitude spectra before/after deghosting; notches should be filled.
- **Well tie:** critical to validate broadband results; check if low-frequency content improves tie.
- **Inversion:** broadband data should reduce dependence on a priori model.

### 7. Onshore broadband (pp. 40–46)

- **Vibroseis capability:** modern vibrators can generate low frequencies (down to 1.5 Hz); challenge is receiver coupling and noise.
- **Alternative sources:** dynamite, airguns (on land) can generate broader bandwidth.
- **Receiver response:** geophone vs. accelerometer; geophone has resonance frequency (~10 Hz) that limits low-frequency response; accelerometer has flat response down to DC.
- **High frequencies on land:** limited by source (vibrator bandwidth) and receiver (geophone resonance).
- **Low-frequency inversion on land:** full-waveform inversion (FWI) requires low frequencies (below 3–5 Hz); challenging on land due to noise and source limitations.

### 8. Implications for survey design (pp. 50–51)

- **Marine:** choose deghosting method based on target (shallow vs. deep), budget, and acquisition constraints.
- **Land:** focus on source and receiver optimization; consider FWI requirements.
- **Hybrid:** OBC/OBN can provide dual-sensor deghosting (like GeoStreamer).

### 9. Measuring broadband quality (pp. 51–53)

- **Octave count:** number of octaves with usable S/N.
- **Notch depth:** how deep are the ghost notches (before deghosting)?
- **Bandwidth extension:** how much low-frequency content is recovered?
- **Validation:** well tie, inversion results, interpretation consistency.

## Key equations

**Ghost notch frequency:**
$$F_N = \frac{1500 \cdot N}{2D}, \quad N = 0, 1, 2, ...$$

**Wavelet lobe width:**
$$L_w \propto \frac{2}{F_{\max} - F_{\min}}$$

**Octave definition:**
$$\text{Number of octaves} = \log_2\left(\frac{F_{\max}}{F_{\min}}\right)$$

## Practical implications

- **Marine processing:** deghosting is essential for broadband; choose method based on acquisition type (conventional streamer, dual-sensor, variable-depth).
- **Land processing:** focus on source/receiver optimization; broadband is achievable but more challenging.
- **QC:** always validate with well synthetics; check octave count and notch depth.
- **Interpretation:** broadband data improves fault tracking, impedance discrimination, and inversion accuracy.

## Implications for teaching

- Excellent overview of broadband concept, benefits, and acquisition techniques.
- Clear explanation of ghost notch physics and commercial solutions.
- Emphasizes importance of low frequencies (not just high frequencies) for resolution.
- Good bridge between acquisition and processing.
